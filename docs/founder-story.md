## The true origin

App Composer is the product of an accumulated way of building, rather than a retrospective AI-founder origin myth.

- **2004–2010:** Served in the **Royal Australian Navy**. The lasting context for the story is working with communications, systems and operations; do not add unapproved service details.
- **2010–2019:** Worked across desktop support, networks and systems.
- **From 2019:** DevOps, including AWS, Ruby on Rails and Heroku-to-AWS migrations.
- **Overlapping music and software work:** Music led to WordPress and WooCommerce, online stores for **Shake Down Records** and **Grind Mode Cypher**, and helping with social channels. This is the personal creator bridge into building software; it overlapped rather than replacing the DevOps work.
- **The original three-tier practice:** (1) reuse good plugins/components, including lifetime licences when worthwhile; (2) pay for specialist or occasional capabilities rather than reproducing them; (3) build the missing parts. Knowing the right GitHub repository at the right moment was part of the same approach, before and after learning to code.
- **AI agents:** Expand the amount of useful work one person can initiate, but do not remove the need for product judgement, references, taste, technical review, permissions, testing and release evidence.
- **App Composer:** Productises that approach for creators, developers and teams: find and reuse suitable parts, direct authorised agents to build what is missing, and own, operate and improve the result. It aims beyond installing existing software to creating things that do not yet exist. The aspiration is not a claim that all modes or one-click deployment already work.

## Public naming and privacy boundaries

The founder has explicitly approved **Royal Australian Navy**, **Shake Down Records** and **Grind Mode Cypher** as named background organisations. App Composer itself is named as the product. Appropriate technical products and platforms (for example WordPress, WooCommerce, GitHub, Ruby on Rails, Heroku, AWS and Cloudflare) can be named as factual tools or infrastructure when relevant to actual work, not as endorsements.

**Update — 2026/11/19:** The founder also permits named organisations, clients and roles from his **own publicly presented LinkedIn employment history**, where relevant to the story. Specifically approved examples: **Unisys, Thales, SchoolStatus, UXC Connect, DXC Connect, NAB (as a contracting client)** and **PlayHQ**. Other employers or client names can be used when genuinely present in the founder's published LinkedIn history or separately confirmed by him; do not guess names, dates, projects, outcomes or employment relationships. Distinguish direct employment, contracting organisations, their clients and projects accurately. Never imply employer, client or platform endorsement of App Composer or reveal confidential work by naming a public employer.

**Editorial judgement:** The canonical story is about the founder's approach to making things — music storefronts, practical reuse, DevOps, agents and ownership — **not a CV or a logo wall**. Keep specific professional names only when they substantively illuminate that development or a credible use case, and prefer a natural account of the work over a catalogue of employers.

**Still private:** Names of coworkers, private correspondents, personal contacts and collaborators who are not themselves relevant, separately public participants; private messages/screenshots, employer/client confidential information, personal circumstances and third-party remarks. Do not expose these merely because a workplace is publicly listed. Do not invent career achievements, dates, audience numbers, metrics or heroic claims. Keep the first-person account approachable, lightly self-deprecating and specific.

## How this appears in the product

- **Canonical public story:** `/story`, linked from the homepage workflow and the Explore Composer resource group.
- **Product consistency:** The three-tier habit explains the existing LEGO-like catalogue, typed capability boundaries, independent app ownership, creative references and agent-led creation. It should influence future beginner copy and product examples rather than become a repeated tagline on every screen.
- **Nearby proof:** Link to `/discover` for currently usable tools, `/building` for a bounded source-backed implementation walkthrough, and `/learn` for a supported starting path.
- **Separate claims:** Distinguish the author's *motivation and intent* from what is demonstrably shipped. The release record and independently maintained app sources own evidence. No fabricated “built entirely by AI” story, universal one-click claim or false technical equivalence.

When editing the story, preserve the actual music-to-software connection and overlapping chronology. Review the public page at phone and desktop widths, the metadata and the live production route before declaring the change shipped.
